Before anything else: these videos are silent

All 33 shorts render with no audio stream at all — deliberately, so sound can be chosen per platform. But a silent upload is close to the worst thing you can hand TikTok or Instagram: audio is a first-class distribution signal on both, and trending audio is one of the few levers a zero-follower account actually has.

So: add sound in the app, every time, before you post. Open the draft in TikTok/Instagram, attach a trending track at low volume, then publish. YouTube Shorts is the one place silence is survivable — and even there the API uploads land unlisted, so you have to flip each one to public in Studio by hand.

The one-paragraph strategy

Video is the whole growth engine. Under about 5K followers, Reels are effectively the only Instagram format that reaches non-followers at all, and TikTok is the strongest platform for new accounts by a distance. So the 33 shorts are the backbone and the 30 memes are supporting material.

One asset, four platforms. Every short goes out natively to YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and X — four independent rolls of the dice on the same content. Always upload the original file: a visible watermark from another platform makes a Reel ineligible for recommendation and costs 40–70% of reach.

Pace is set by supply, not stamina. 33 shorts at one a day is five weeks. Two a day would burn the library in under three weeks and then go silent, which is strictly worse — so this runs daily and stops when the shelf is empty.

Read the clock as a tiebreaker, not a lever. The next section works through why — short version: with no followers yet the exact hour is close to noise on TikTok, Reels and Shorts, so this calendar defaults to two sittings a day rather than six scattered alarms.

Does the exact time actually matter?

Right now, on TikTok / Reels / Shorts: barely. The mechanism that makes timing matter is the follower test — the platform shows a new video to your existing followers first and uses their response to decide whether to push it wider. With zero followers that step is skipped entirely: your video goes straight to a small interest-matched pool of strangers, assembled when the algorithm gets to it, not when you hit publish. So 11:30 versus 18:00 is close to noise.

What actually decides it is watch time and completion rate — together roughly 40–50% of TikTok's ranking, with the bar for wide distribution around 70% completion. Your shorts are 25–33s, which is the right length. Whether someone watches to the end is the whole ballgame, and a silent upload loses that fight in the first second.

Where time genuinely matters: X, which is recency-driven and decays in hours. And later — once you have a real follower base, the follower-test step switches on and the staggered clock starts earning its keep. That's why both schedules are here.

So: two sittings a day is the honest default. It costs you almost nothing in reach and you'll actually do it. Keep the two X posts about 30 minutes apart so they aren't competing with each other, and stay near your phone for the first hour after posting — early replies are a real signal, and that beats any clock.

What differs per platform

Instagram Never post a meme as a single image

Single images sit near 0.35% engagement with no non-follower distribution. Grouped into carousels of three they run ~0.55% and earn roughly 9× the saves. That's why the 30 memes appear as 10 themed carousels, never as 30 posts.

TikTok Carousels are filler, video is the point

Video beats photo mode on essentially every metric here, so the carousels run on Tue/Thu only and never replace a video day.

X The one place text beats image

Text-only posts edge images (~3.56% vs 3.40% median engagement) and video pulls roughly 10× text. Hence three posts a day: text at 09:00, image at 16:30, video at 20:00. Without Premium you need roughly 4–8× the engagement for the same reach — so treat X as a compounding side bet, not the growth engine.

YouTube No Community posts yet

The 500-subscriber gate was removed in 2023, so you can post them — but they're distributed to subscribers, so at zero subs they reach nobody. Revisit at ~500 subs. Also: Shorts read the first line of the description as a search signal, so use the YT caption from the kit rather than pasting the Instagram one.

Hashtags: 3–5, not 30

Instagram dropped hashtag following in 2024 and says hashtags don't meaningfully lift visibility — keyword-rich captions do. TikTok has stated #fyp and #foryou do not influence the algorithm. The kit's captions carry 6–7 tags; trim to the 3–5 most specific before posting.

When it runs out

Week 5 empties the library. Have the next batch rendering by week 3 — the single worst outcome for reach is a strong five-week run followed by silence. If you're not ready, repost your two best performers rather than going dark, but keep it under ten reposts a month: past that, accounts get dropped from recommendations.
